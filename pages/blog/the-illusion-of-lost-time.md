---
title: The Illusion of Lost Time
date: 2026/9/21
description: On lost time, the quiet pressure to justify every minute, and the kind of living that leaves no trace.
tag: time, productivity, rest, reflection, self
author: Alihan Ekiz
---

We talk about lost time as if we misplaced it somewhere.

As if it were still lying there, in some place we simply forgot.

As if we could go back and get it.

But time doesn't disappear. It just becomes something else.

An afternoon where nothing happened becomes memory. A year that felt pointless becomes the person you are today. None of it is gone. It has only turned into you, without a receipt, without proof.

We count hours as if they were money.

Spent. Invested. Wasted.

But time isn't an account. It can't be saved, set aside, postponed to later.

It flows, whether you use it or not.

The person staring out a window for hours loses nothing. They live just as much as the one who finishes a book in that same stretch of time.

Only the story we tell afterward is different.

There's a quiet pressure to justify every minute.

As if rest were a theft.

As if doing nothing were a debt you have to repay later.

But maybe that's exactly the lie.

The idea that time only counts when it visibly produces something.

That a walk with no destination is worth less than one that leads to work.

That a conversation with no purpose matters less than one that accomplishes something.

Some of the most important hours in life leave no trace.

No result. No photo. No proof they ever happened.

And yet they were there.

Still, they shaped you, in a way that can't be measured.

Maybe lost time isn't a category of time at all.

Maybe it's only a category of expectation.

A standard we imposed on ourselves, to translate life into productivity, because we're afraid it wouldn't mean anything otherwise.

But life doesn't owe anyone meaning in every single minute.

Some time is simply lived.

Not used. Not optimized. Not justified.

Just lived.

And maybe, in the end, that's the only time that really counts.
